Your task

In two days, build a game that someone else can sit down and play.

You decide what game: any genre, as long as it runs in a browser from your team's GitHub Pages address. You get 3 hours on day 1 and 2 hours on day 2. Agree on your idea before day 1, and pick something small enough that it is playable by the end of day 1.

Your tools

  • One Claude Pro login, sent to you by email. Log in at claude.ai/code and test it before day 1; there is no setup in the room.
  • Claude Code writes the code. You decide what to build, instruct it clearly, check what it makes and steer it when it goes wrong.
  • One public GitHub repo with Pages: every push publishes the game at your address.
  • Any language or framework that gives static files for a browser. The internet is allowed.

Rules

  1. No Saab code, documents or data, and nothing classified. Public information and made-up data only. Your repo is public: anyone can see what you push.
  2. Art, music and sound are made by you or Claude, or are free to use.
  3. Claude Code writes most of the code. Say in the demo what you did by hand.
  4. Building stops on the minute, both days. No building overnight.
  5. The demo is 5 minutes, live, at your address, then 1 minute for questions. Have it ready.
  6. After the demos everyone plays at each team's address.

Working with Claude Code

  • Start small. Get the simplest version running, then add one thing at a time.
  • Give it the goal, not just the next step: who plays and what makes it fun.
  • Plan first on big steps: "Make a plan before you write code."
  • Play it after every merge at your address, and tell Claude what you saw.
  • Publish often. Merge into main after every working step.
  • Describe what you see when it fails: the error, or what the screen shows.
  • Share the steering. Take turns writing the instructions.

Your repo and how to publish

Your repo is github.com/vibe-jam-2026/team-9 and your game is published at vibe-jam-2026.github.io/team-9. You are reading this page there now: your game replaces it. These instructions stay at vibe-jam-2026.github.io/team-9/instructions.html.

How to publish

  1. Send Adrian your GitHub usernames before day 1. He adds you to your team's repo with write access.
  2. Open claude.ai/code in the browser, log in with your team's login and pick the repo vibe-jam-2026/team-9.
  3. The game starts from index.html at the top of the repo. Everything the browser needs must be in the repo as static files: HTML, JavaScript, CSS, images and sound.
  4. Claude pushes its work to a branch of its own. Publish by asking it to open a pull request, then click Merge on github.com, so the change lands in main.
  5. GitHub Pages publishes every change to main by itself. About a minute later the new version is live at your address.
  6. Built with a tool such as Vite? Put the finished files at the top of the repo or in docs/, and tell Adrian if you use docs/. Use relative paths, because your game lives under /team-9/.
Questions during the exercise: ask Adrian.
